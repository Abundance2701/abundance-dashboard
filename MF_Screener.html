How the numbers are calculated (read before using with clients)
  • Category matching uses each fund's SEBI sub-category, not its name. "Include Index Funds" adds all index funds, since SEBI treats them as one category.
  • Returns: under 1 year are absolute; 1Y and longer are CAGR. A fund younger than the period shows "—".
  • Quartiles: each calendar year's return is ranked within the same SEBI sub-category and plan (Regular vs Direct ranked separately). Q1 = top 25%. Needs at least 4 funds with data that year. The latest year is year-to-date.
  • Peer set caveat: only currently active schemes are ranked, so funds that were merged or closed are missing from older years (survivorship bias). SEBI's 2017–18 recategorisation also means some funds ran a different mandate before 2018 — treat 2016–2018 ranks with care.
  • Risk ratios follow Value Research's published approach: month-end NAVs for the trailing 3 (or 5) years up to the last completed month. Std Dev is annualised. Sharpe and Sortino use the risk-free rate you enter; Sortino's downside counts only negative months. Alpha is Jensen's alpha, annualised. Max Drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough fall in daily NAV over the window.
  • Beta & Alpha benchmark: a direct-plan index fund is used as a proxy for the index (Nifty 50 for Large Cap, Midcap 150 for Mid Cap, Smallcap 250 for Small Cap, LargeMidcap 250 for Large & Mid, Nifty 500 for Flexi/Multi/Focused/Value/ELSS/Sectoral/Aggressive Hybrid). Proxy returns sit slightly below the TRI due to the index fund's expense ratio. Debt and other hybrids show no Beta/Alpha.
  • vs Index: every 3-year period ending at each month-end (up to 10 years back) is compared with the benchmark proxy. The proxy is a direct-plan index fund, so Regular-plan funds start about 1% a year behind on costs alone; compare in Direct plan for a cleaner read. Index funds for Midcap 150, Smallcap 250, LargeMidcap 250 and Nifty 500 only began around 2019–21, so those categories have fewer periods. Up/Down capture use monthly returns over the risk window.
  • AUM is the latest AMFI quarterly average per plan, in ₹ Cr.

How to read this

Active share: how different the fund's stocks are from its index. 0% is an index copy, 100% shares nothing. Labels (Abundance rule of thumb, an assumption): below 40% Moves closely with index, 40–60% Moderately active, above 60% Highly active.
Overlap: for every stock two funds both own, take the smaller of the two weights and add these up. 40% overlap means 40% of the money sits in the same stocks in both funds.
Invested in shares: the part of the fund's money in shares. The rest is cash, bonds, or shares hedged with derivatives (counted as not invested here).
Market cap: the official AMFI list (Jan–Jun 2026 average market cap, in force Aug 2026–Jan 2027), the same list AMCs use in factsheets. A few new listings not on it are classed by index membership.
Weights: stock weights (top 5, common holdings, top 10) are % of the whole fund, matching factsheets. Active share, overlap, market-cap mix and sector bets use the share portion only, so funds holding cash are compared fairly.
Index used: Large Cap → Nifty 100, Mid Cap → Nifty Midcap 150, Small Cap → Nifty Smallcap 250, Large & Mid → Nifty LargeMidcap 250, all others → Nifty 500. Index weights from ICICI Pru index fund holdings on 30/09/2026.
Source: official AMC monthly portfolio disclosures; each fund shows its own date. Bandhan and HSBC are not included yet (their holdings files weren't available). Illustrative analysis for internal fund research, not a recommendation of any scheme.
Data: AMFI (via tigzig.com's open NAV/AUM mirror); holdings from AMC monthly portfolio disclosures. Calculated from NAV — always spot-check against the factsheet before using in client conversations.
